## 1. Executive summary

**What we tested.** The real customer path for a large webinar: 1,010 viewers watching through the Bunny CDN, the way a browser would, while 2 presenters were on stage with a screen share, a cloud recording was running, and the audience was chatting, asking questions, upvoting and voting.

**What went well.**

- **The CDN now takes almost all the video load off our server.** In the morning's runs the viewers were (by mistake) going straight to the video box, and 500 of them pushed it to 79-97% CPU. This time, 1,010 viewers through the CDN left the box at **34% CPU**, sending only **5 Mbit/s** instead of 450-570 Mbit/s. The box only had to send about one copy of the stream to the CDN; the CDN fanned it out. That is the result we needed to plan bigger events on one box.
- **Every viewer got in.** All 1,000 viewers on the viewer machines and the 10 on the first machine joined. 2 join attempts got a rate-limit reply and succeeded on retry (see 7.4). The server counted exactly 1,010 viewers at the peak, matching the clients.
- **The API was healthy.** Server-side p95 about 0.2 s, no server errors (apart from 40 expected "video server starting" replies while the box was booting), no database connection errors.
- **Interaction worked at 1,000.** 400 of 400 chat messages accepted and all found in the host's transcript; 40/40 questions; 120/120 upvotes; 3 polls and 2 quizzes with 150 votes each, and every stored tally matched the votes exactly.
- **Last night's memory-leak fix works.** API goroutines rose with viewers and fell back within a minute of them leaving (before #717 they stayed for 25 minutes).

**What did not go well.**

- **At 1,000 viewers, playback broke down for most of the simulated viewers.** Up to 400 viewers, playback was perfect: 0 stalls. At 1,000, the four viewer machines received only about 36% of the video they should have, and when the step ended **723 of the 1,000 viewers were stalled** (their buffer was empty). They saw 417 stalls that ended, plus many that had not ended yet. The errors were requests for the live playlist being refused (`400`, about 227,000 of them), CDN `503`s (about 1,350 on playlists and 850 on video parts), and connection failures and timeouts.
- **We do not yet know whether a real audience would see this.** The video box was fine (34% CPU) and the server's origin was not overloaded. The failure was between the CDN and our test machines. Each test machine sent 250 viewers from **one IP address**, about 3,000 requests per second. Ten viewers on the first machine watched through the same CDN for the whole run with **0 stalls**. That points at something per-machine or per-IP: a CDN rate limit or protection, the CDN caching an error reply, or the test kit's retry behaviour. Real viewers come from many IPs. But a company all-hands behind one office network could look similar.

**Bottom line.** The video box and the API can carry a 1,000-viewer webinar through the CDN with a lot of headroom. The CDN path at 1,000 is **not yet proven**. Before selling 1,000-viewer events we need to (1) find out from Bunny's logs why it refused playlists and returned 503s, (2) fix the CDN settings for live playlists, and (3) re-run 1,000 with the viewers spread over more machines and IPs.

**Next steps, in order:**

1. Pull Bunny's logs for 15:05-15:09 UTC: the status of the `400` and `503` replies, whether they were cache hits, and any rate-limit or protection events.
2. Make sure Bunny never caches error replies for playlists, and review its settings for blocking playlist requests (section 7).
3. Re-run 1,000 viewers spread over 8 or more generators (125 or fewer each), and a 250-viewer single-IP control.
4. Raise the kit's presenter wait for a cold box to 5 minutes in the repo (done locally for this run).
5. Check that the cloud recording from this run plays back.

### 5.3 Bunny

The dashboard showed, for the month: 29 GB, 2.86 M requests, **cache HIT 6.5%**.

**Why 6.5% does not mean the CDN is failing.**

- It is a monthly number. It includes the earlier traffic, when the zone pointed at the old GCP origin, and other test traffic.
- In LL-HLS, most requests are blocking playlist reloads (`_HLS_msn` / `_HLS_part`). Each one is a new URL, so with query-string vary on, every one of them starts out as a miss. Requests that Bunny **coalesces** into one origin fetch are most likely also counted as misses, even though only one of them reaches the origin.
- Parts are fetched within a second of being published, mostly while the first fetch is still in flight. That is coalescing again, not a classic cache hit.

**The metric that matters is origin egress**: MediaMTX HLS out, or box network out, against the audience's total. Here that was 1.2 Mbit/s against 411-456 Mbit/s delivered. **Recommendation:** for every large event, record MediaMTX HLS out and box network out next to the viewer count. If origin egress starts to grow with viewers, coalescing or caching has stopped working. Do not use Bunny's hit ratio as the health signal for live streams.

## 7. Issues found and recommendations, ranked

### 7.1 [High] LL-HLS playlists fail through the CDN at 250 viewers per source IP / 1,000 total

**Symptoms (step 3):**

- 227,000 `playlist 400`s, about one per viewer per second, the kit's retry rate.
- 7,541 "no new media for 30 s".
- 1,349 playlist and 849 part `503`s from Bunny.
- 3,965 playlist connection errors and 984 playlist timeouts.
- 723 of 1,000 viewers stalled when the step ended.
- About 36% of the expected video delivered.

**What we know:**

- **It is not the box.** Box CPU was 34%, origin egress 1.2 Mbit/s. Viewers that were receiving parts got them in about 130 ms at p95.
- **It is not generator CPU.** Loop p99 was 48 ms or less.
- **MediaMTX normally answers an old blocking request at once.** It returns `400` only when `_HLS_msn` / `_HLS_part` are *ahead* of what it can serve. A viewer that retries the same URL a second later is by then asking for the past, and the origin would answer `200`. A viewer that keeps getting `400` on the same URL for minutes is therefore most likely being answered by **the CDN**, not the origin.
- **Origin-direct, the same kit had far fewer `400`s.** In runs 4-5, one generator sent 500 viewers straight to the box and saw 781-829 `400`s, not tens of thousands.
- **lg-1's 10 CDN viewers had 0 stalls through the same period.** The failure followed the generators that each sent 250 viewers, about 3,000 requests per second, from one IP. It did not follow the stream.
- At 100 per generator (400 total) it was clean, apart from 173 `400`s on lg-2.

**Hypotheses, most likely first:**

1. **Bunny caches the `400` reply** for a blocking playlist URL. Everyone who asks for that URL, including every retry, then gets the cached error until it expires. A first `400` can come from a request racing the live edge at a part boundary; lg-1's 118 show that this happens even at low load. With the coalescing and query-vary changes, one such `400` would be multiplied.
2. **Bunny's per-IP protection or rate limiting** (pull-zone limits, or DDoS protection) acting on 250 viewers behind one IP. That would explain the `503`s, the connection errors and lg-1 being unaffected. Note that real viewers behind one corporate NAT (an all-hands) are a real version of this.
3. **Coalescing and blocking requests interact badly.** A coalesced origin fetch for a blocking request that times out or fails hands the same failure to every waiter. Bunny's own timeout may be shorter than the origin's hold, which would give `503`s and timeouts.
4. **The kit's retry is harsher than hls.js.** The kit retries the identical blocking URL every second, forever. hls.js applies its own retry and back-off policy, and may reload without the delivery directives. If so, a browser would recover from a stuck URL where the kit does not. This would make the kit overstate the impact, but it would not explain the `503`s.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Bunny logs for 15:05-15:09 UTC.** For the `400`s and `503`s, record the cache status (HIT or MISS), the edge server, and whether the origin was contacted. Also check the pull zone's security and rate-limit events for the four generator IPs. This one step tells hypotheses 1-3 apart.
2. **Do not cache error replies for `.m3u8`.** Add an edge rule: playlist responses with 4xx/5xx status get cache time 0, and preferably playlists in general use origin cache-control only. Keep caching parts and segments.
3. **Review the blocking-reload settings.**
   - Consider excluding playlists (or blocking playlist requests) from request coalescing while keeping it for parts. Origin egress for playlists is tiny anyway.
   - Make Bunny's origin timeout longer than MediaMTX's longest blocking hold (at least 3 × target duration plus margin).
   - Revisit the part duration (500 ms). Longer parts mean fewer playlist reloads per viewer.
4. **Check how hls.js behaves** when a blocking playlist reload returns `400` or `503` (retry count, back-off, whether it drops `_HLS_msn` / `_HLS_part`). Then make the kit do the same, so its stall numbers mean what a browser would see. A browser smoke test of 1-2 tabs during the next 1,000 step would confirm it directly.
5. **Re-run 1,000 with more source IPs**: 8 or more generators at 125 or fewer, as well as a control of 250 from one IP. If 125-per-IP is clean, the remaining risk is shared-NAT audiences; ask Bunny about per-IP limits.

**Impact if it is real for browsers:** most of an audience above about 400 would freeze for many seconds at a time. Treat 1,000-viewer events as **not ready** until this is explained.

### 7.2 [Medium] Bunny cache hit ratio is not a useful live metric

See 5.3. Track **origin egress per event** (MediaMTX HLS out and box network out against viewers), and alert if it grows with the audience.

### 7.3 [Medium] Cold box: about 4 minutes to the first presenter

But a host who goes live at the last minute makes the first viewers wait. Pre-warm the box for scheduled events. Raise the kit's wait permanently (section 8).

### 7.4 [Low] A few 429s at the 1,000 step
